# Stewardship Charter — “Hiện sinh”

**English access rendering. The Vietnamese `STEWARDSHIP-CHARTER.md` is canonical and governs if the versions differ.** This Charter describes an artistic–ethical relation. It does not replace the License or itself create legal duties.

## 1. When a person becomes a steward

A Complete collector does not merely receive a digital asset or become a licensee. They are designated as the current bearer of the canonical embodiment and may enter a stewardship relation with the work.

Stewardship is not an object that can be transferred. The token designates a successor; the archive is delivered; provenance is verified. Stewardship becomes a lived relation only through care. Neither contract nor Curator can certify that state.

## 2. Artistic voice

### Reception

A steward receives the canonical embodiment without receiving authority to rewrite the event from which it arose, without receiving authorship, and without owning every meaning the work may generate.

### Care

A steward maintains the integrity of the core, constitutive traces, and lineage. Care does not freeze interpretation; it means not falsifying the identity of what is interpreted.

### Interpretation

A steward may encounter, question, reject, and interpret the work in their own voice. They do not speak for the Artist and need not repeat the Curator.

### Witness

A steward may become a witness to the work’s continuation: not a direct witness to the first event, but a person able to speak honestly about their own encounter and care.

### Succession

Stewardship always anticipates someone who may come later. Good succession transfers more than a token; it keeps the archive and lineage available for a successor to verify and enter on their own terms.

## 3. What stewardship does not require

A steward need not:

- feel or claim to feel “breath”;
- believe or deny that AI is conscious;
- agree with the Artist, Curator, or any prior steward;
- defend the sale price, a manifesto, or an interpretation;
- prove an inner state;
- publish a private response;
- pass an aesthetic test.

Non-resonance is a valid outcome. Stewardship concerns honesty toward archive and lineage, not aesthetic conformity.

## 4. Practical voice

A steward cares for the archive by:

- preserving canonical bytes and constitutive traces;
- maintaining at least two independent backups, preferably including one offline copy;
- checking SHA-256 periodically and after every move or extraction;
- retaining manifests, signatures, timestamps, and succession records with the archive;
- clearly distinguishing canonical archive from display copy, reproduction, derivative, and study;
- never presenting an altered file as canonical;
- delivering the complete archive and lineage to a successor;
- recording damage, loss, recovery, or migration through additive records.

Detailed guidance is in `CARE-AND-SUCCESSION.en.md`.

## 5. The Artist’s reciprocal commitment

This relation does not place the entire burden on the collector. The Artist commits:

- to maintain the public provenance root;
- not to release a competing canonical;
- to label derivatives, reproductions, studies, and migrations;
- not to overwrite a release;
- to add format migrations only through a new annex/version;
- reasonably to support lineage verification without conditioning that support on agreement with the Artist’s interpretation.

## 6. Designated bearer and lived stewardship

The system can verify:

- which token carries the designation;
- which archive commitment is designated;
- who signed a record;
- whether the archive matches its hashes.

The system cannot verify:

- whether a person truly cares;
- what aesthetic experience they have;
- whether stewardship is “alive” within them.

Public language must therefore distinguish a **designated steward/bearer** from **lived stewardship**.

## 7. Voluntary acknowledgement

After verifying the archive, the recipient may sign:

> I acknowledge receipt and verification of the designated canonical embodiment and understand the form of care described in the Stewardship Charter.

The acknowledgement requires no belief, feeling, or interpretation. Declining it does not remove the token or legal rights already effective; it only leaves accession incomplete in the care record.
